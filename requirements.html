1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the user requirements and system requirements for NoiseMap NYC, a predictive noise‑mapping system designed for apartment renters in New York City.

The system is needed because renters typically evaluate neighborhoods based only on current noise conditions, which do not reflect how noise levels may change due to future construction, traffic variations, or seasonal activity. Chronic exposure to noise above 55 dB affects health and reduces rental value, making noise a significant decision factor. NoiseMap NYC addresses this need by offering forward‑looking, data‑driven noise predictions.

1.2 System Overview

NoiseMap NYC is a web‑based system that predicts neighborhood noise levels 6 to 12 months into the future for every 50 x 50 meter grid across New York City.

The system integrates multiple urban datasets—including SONYC sensor readings, construction permits, traffic counts, zoning data, and OpenStreetMap features—and processes them through a machine learning model.

To support transparency and align with the textbook’s emphasis on clear system definition, the system provides explainable outputs, identifying the contribution of construction, traffic, and baseline urban activity to each prediction.

The final output is delivered through an interactive map interface that displays predicted noise levels, source breakdowns, and a confidence indicator.

1.3 Intended Audience

  • Apartment renters: Users evaluating unfamiliar neighbourhoods and seeking reliable, forward‑looking information about noise conditions before signing a lease.
  • Urban planners and city officials: Need predictive insights into how construction activity, traffic patterns, and zoning changes will affect neighbourhood soundscapes.
  • First‑time renters: Require understandable noise information to support housing decisions.
  • Development team: Responsible for developing and maintaining the system while managing requirement changes.

1.4 Scope

  • Provide predicted neighbourhood noise levels based on user-input addresses
  • Predict future decibel (dB) levels for selected time and lease periods
  • Generate temporal noise forecast visualisations through interactive line graphs
  • Analyse the contribution of major noise sources using SHAP
  • Display prediction confidence indicators
  • Enable parallel comparison of multiple neighbourhoods
  • Calculate noise exposure overlap during the lease period
  • Provide estimated variations in noise exposure according to apartment floor level

System will NOT do

  • Not fully predict unpredictable human activities
  • Not provide real-time indoor noise monitoring
  • Not guarantee future noise outcomes
2. User Requirements Definition

2.1 Stakeholders

Primary Users

  • Apartment Renters & First-time Renters
    Goal: Understand future noise conditions before signing a lease
    Concerns: Accuracy, simplicity, plain language

Secondary Users

  • Urban Researchers
    Goal: Understand neighbourhood noise patterns
    Concerns: Data transparency, model reliability

External Data Providers

  • NYC Department of Buildings
  • NYC Liquor Activity Licenses
  • NYC DOT Traffic Counts
  • SONYC Acoustic Sensor Network

Internal Stakeholders

  • Development Team
    Goal: Build system within scope and schedule
    Concerns: Clear requirements, feasibility, data availability

2.2 User Requirement Statements

  • UR1. System shall allow a user to enter an address and receive noise predictions up to 12 months.
  • UR2. System shall display predictions in simple language.
  • UR3. System shall identify dominant noise sources using badges.
  • UR4. System shall allow users to enter lease duration and view noise changes.
  • UR5. System shall allow comparison of two neighbourhoods.
  • UR6. System shall indicate prediction reliability.
  • UR7. System should communicate temporary noise (e.g., construction end date).
  • UR8. System will rely only on public open-source data.
  • UR9. System should reference WHO 55 dB health thresholds.
  • UR10. System shall allow floor-level noise adjustment.
  • UR11. System shall estimate financial impact of noise above 55 dB.

2.3 Constraints

Operational Constraints

  • Web-based application
  • Accessible on desktop and mobile
  • No login required
  • No installation or plugins required
  • Free to use

Data Constraints

  • Only public open data sources
  • No personal data stored
  • Predictions not guaranteed
  • Confidence indicator required
  • 311 complaint bias acknowledged
  • All data sources must be attributed

Scope Constraints

  • No real-time monitoring
  • NYC only
  • No indoor noise estimates
  • 12-month prediction limit
  • No neighbour noise prediction
3. System Requirements Specification

3.1 Functional Requirements

The following requirements define the specific services and functions the system shall provide.

  • FR1. The system shall generate predicted noise levels in decibels for every 50x50 metre grid block across New York City for a forward-looking horizon of up to 12 months.
  • FR2. The system shall apply a predictive model trained on historical urban data to generate monthly noise level predictions per grid block.
  • FR3. The system shall decompose each prediction into individual noise source contributions covering construction, traffic and baseline urban activity.
  • FR4. The system shall compute a confidence score for each grid block based on SONYC sensor proximity and the availability of approved permit data, classifying each as High, Medium or Low.
  • FR5. The system shall distinguish between temporary noise sources with a defined permit end date and permanent noise sources such as traffic in all prediction outputs.
  • FR6. The system shall flag grid blocks where current noise conditions appear low but approved nearby construction permits indicate a significant predicted increase within the forecast period.
  • FR7. The system shall render an interactive web map colour coded by predicted noise level for each grid block.
  • FR8. The system shall display a noise timeline forecast as a line graph for any user-selected grid block, showing monthly predicted noise levels across the forecast horizon.
  • FR9. The system shall display noise source contributions as labelled badges for any selected grid block.
  • FR10. The system shall display a confidence indicator of High, Medium or Low for each grid block reflecting the quality and availability of underlying data.
  • FR11. The system shall visually distinguish grid blocks whose predictions are derived primarily from sensor data from those estimated using complaint proxy data.
  • FR12. The system shall provide a lease overlap calculator that accepts a move-in date and lease duration as inputs and returns a summarised noise profile covering that specific tenancy period.
  • FR13. The system shall display a financial impact estimate within the lease calculator, translating predicted decibel levels above 55 dB into an approximate rental value reduction based on established research.
  • FR14. The system shall allow simultaneous side-by-side comparison of predicted noise levels across at least two neighbourhoods.
  • FR15. The system shall allow users to input their apartment floor level and return an adjusted noise estimate reflecting vertical sound attenuation.
  • FR16. The system shall allow users to filter or highlight grid blocks based on a personally defined noise tolerance threshold.
  • FR17. The system shall generate a shareable output of noise predictions for any selected location.

3.2 Non-Functional Requirements

The following non-functional requirements define the quality attributes and constraints the system must satisfy. They are organised in three categories: product, organisational, and external.

Product Requirements

  • NFR1. Noise level classifications shall present moderate noise as a neutral condition, acknowledging that some users actively prefer a degree of ambient noise.
  • NFR2. The system shall communicate the significance of noise exposure to first-time users prior to their engagement with the map.
  • NFR3. The system shall explicitly state that noise from neighbouring residents and building sound insulation quality are outside its predictive scope.
  • NFR4. The system shall differentiate between predictions derived from sensor data and those estimated from complaint proxy data, enabling users to assess prediction reliability accordingly.
  • NFR5. The system shall distinguish between temporary noise sources such as construction and permanent ones such as traffic in all user-facing outputs.

Organisational Requirements

  • NFR6. The system shall be developed open source tools and frameworks only.
  • NFR7. The geographic scope of the system is limited to New York City in its initial version.
  • NFR8. The prediction horizon is fixed at a maximum of 12 months.
  • NFR9. The spatial resolution of the prediction grid is fixed at 50x50 metres.
  • NFR10. The predictive model shall be validated against a held-out historical test dataset prior to deployment.

External Requirements

  • NFR11. The system shall reference peer-reviewed research studies and verified user feedback to substantiate the reliability of its noise predictions.
  • NFR12. The system shall account for known biases in NYC 311 complaint data, recognising that complaint rates may reflect reporting behaviour rather than objective noise levels.
  • NFR13. Noise data shall be presented at grid block level only to prevent identification of individual residents, in accordance with user privacy expectations.

3.3 Domain Requirements

  • DR1. The system shall adopt decibels (dB) as the standard unit of noise measurement, consistent with established acoustic conventions.
  • DR2. The system shall designate 55 dB as the threshold above which noise is considered potentially harmful, in accordance with World Health Organisation environmental noise guidelines.
  • DR3. Noise level differences shall be communicated in a manner that reflects the non-linear nature of human noise perception.
  • DR4. The system shall model traffic, construction and baseline urban activity as the primary noise categories, consistent with established urban acoustic research.
  • DR5. Construction noise shall be treated as time-bounded based on NYC DOB permit duration, while traffic noise shall be treated as a continuous time-varying source consistent with NYC DOT traffic count patterns.
  • DR6. The system shall rely exclusively on publicly available open datasets, consistent with NYC Open Data policy and open government data principles.
  • DR7. NYC DOB permit filings shall be treated as records of approved construction intent rather than guaranteed execution.
  • DR8. NYC 311 complaint data shall be treated as a citizen-reported proxy rather than a direct noise measurement, consistent with known limitations of complaint-based data in urban informatics research.
  • DR9. All noise predictions shall be framed within the context of residential rental decision-making, recognising that renters evaluate noise within a defined tenancy window.
4. System Evolution

4.1 Current Assumptions

The following assumptions underpin the current design and requirements of the system. If any of these assumptions prove incorrect, requirements may need to be revised accordingly.

  • A1. SONYC sensor data from 55 sensors is assumed to provide sufficient ground truth coverage to train a model that generalises across the broader NYC grid.
  • A2. NYC DOB permit filings are assumed to be a reliable indicator of future construction noise activity within the forecast period.
  • A3. Monthly temporal aggregation is assumed to be sufficiently granular for renter decision-making, as renters are assumed to plan by month rather than by week or day.
  • A4. The primary users of the system are assumed to be non-technical apartment renters who require plain language outputs without acoustic expertise.
  • A5. Desktop browser access is assumed to be the primary mode of interaction, with mobile support deferred to a future version.
  • A6. All required data sources are assumed to remain publicly available and consistently updated throughout the system lifecycle.
  • A7. SHAP-derived noise source contributions are assumed to be translatable into plain language labels that are meaningful and interpretable by non-specialist users.
  • A8. Construction permits are assumed to represent approved intent rather than guaranteed execution, and predictions based on permit data are presented accordingly.

4.2 Anticipated Changes

  • AC1. Geographic scope may be expanded beyond New York City to other major urban centres as the data pipeline and model architecture are validated.
  • AC2. Additional noise predictors may be incorporated including nightlife density such as proximity to nightclubs and entertainment venues, garbage collection routes and proximity to elevated subway tracks.
  • AC3. Mobile browser support may be introduced in a future version following validation of the desktop experience.
  • AC4. Floor-level noise attenuation may be incorporated as a model variable in a future version.
  • AC5. The confidence indicator may be refined as additional SONYC sensor data becomes available.
  • AC6. A notification or alert feature may be introduced to inform users of significant predicted noise changes for saved locations.
  • AC7. User review integration may be considered in a future version to supplement data-driven predictions with community-sourced noise reports.
Appendix A — Requirements Elicitation

A.1 Scope of Elicitation

The elicitation activities were designed to answer the following questions:

  • How do renters currently evaluate neighbourhood noise before signing a lease?
  • How important is noise relative to other rental decision factors?
  • What noise sources concern renters most?
  • Whether renters would trust a predictive noise tool and under what conditions?
  • What information renters wish they had known before signing their lease?

A.2 Elicitation Methods and Participants

Survey

  • Format: Online structured questionnaire with branching logic
  • Respondents: 28 valid responses
  • Demographics: 27 aged 18–25, 1 aged over 35; 18 male, 10 female
  • 20 with renting experience, 8 with no renting experience

Interviews

  • Format: Semi-structured (in-person and video call)
  • Participants: 12 interviewees conducted by 6 team members
  • Locations: Japan, Thailand, China, United States
  • Focus areas: noise experience, health awareness, information gaps

A.3 Key Findings

  • Information Asymmetry: Most respondents relied on a single visit; many experienced louder-than-expected noise.
  • Health Impact Confirmed: 50% unaware of WHO 55 dB threshold; common impacts include sleep disruption and stress.
  • Conditional Trust: 40% willing to trust predictions; trust increases when data sources are explained.
  • Awareness Gap: No participant knew construction permits are publicly available before construction begins.

A.4 Open Issues

  • Availability of nightlife and building insulation datasets for NYC
  • Feasibility of time-of-day prediction granularity
  • Adequacy of SONYC sensor coverage for full NYC grid calibration

Appendix C — Interview Guide

Interviews were semi-structured and conducted across three focus areas. Interviewers were encouraged to adapt questions based on the participant's responses and renting experience.

Focus Area 1: Noise Experience

  • Can you tell me about where you currently live or have lived previously?
  • Have you ever experienced any noise problems at a place you were renting?
  • When did you first realise noise was going to be a problem — before or after signing?
  • What was the main source of the noise?

Focus Area 2: Health and Awareness

  • How did the noise affect your daily life?
  • Were you aware that prolonged noise exposure above 55 decibels has been linked to cardiovascular disease and sleep disorders?

Focus Area 3: Information Gap

  • Before moving in, did you try to check the noise level of the area at all — and if so, how?
  • Were you aware that approved construction permits are publicly available before construction begins?
  • If you had known the noise situation in advance, would it have changed your decision?
  • What information about noise would you have wanted before signing your lease?
